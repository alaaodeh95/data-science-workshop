SLIDE 2: Words need context
[2 minutes]

SAY: "If I only show you the word bank, you do not know which meaning I intend. Nearby words help."
Read the two sentences. Ask which words are useful: river and deposited money.
The point is not that attention alone solves word sense or that these are actual model outputs. These are conceptual examples.
A token representation is a vector of numbers. Contextualizing it means updating that vector using information from other positions. We are not generating a new word yet.
TRANSITION: "How can a model bring that useful information together?"

SLIDE 3: From carrying memory to reading context
[2 minutes]

SAY: "An LSTM carries and updates memory as it processes tokens. Its gates help retain information. Attention gives each position a direct way to combine information from other allowed positions."
The diagrams show information routes, not measured model weights.
Do not say LSTMs cannot remember. Their sequential dependency is the bridge to today's topic.
In an encoder, positions can usually read both sides. A causal decoder has restrictions that we will show later.
TRANSITION: "That lookup has three ingredients. Their names sound harder than the idea."

SLIDE 4: Query, key, value: think of a library
[3 minutes]

SAY: "Imagine choosing a recipe. Your query is a request: something warm for lunch. A key describes an item so it can be matched: soup, hot, lunch. The value is the actual information retrieved: the recipe content."
Keys and values play different roles. Query and key determine how much; value determines what is contributed.
In real attention all three are numeric vectors, not English descriptions. The analogy explains the roles, not the actual implementation.
Ask: "Which do we compare with the query: the key or the value?" Answer: key.
TRANSITION: "After matching, how do we combine information?"

=================================================================

SLIDE 5: Attention is a weighted blend
[3 minutes]

SAY: "The request is compared with the keys. Matching scores become proportions through softmax. Those proportions blend the value vectors."
The weights on this slide are invented for teaching. They are not measured recipe-model output. All three sum to 100 percent.
Read the bars: eighty percent of A's value, ten percent of B, ten percent of C. Attention normally creates a blend, not one hard winner.
Do not call the value itself an attention weight. The weight is the mixing amount; the value is the content being mixed.
Ask participants: "If B's weight increased, whose information would influence the result more?"
TRANSITION: "Let's see what the output actually is."

=================================================================

SLIDE 6: The output is a vector, not a word
[3 minutes]

SAY: "These are toy values we chose, not real word embeddings. A supplies [10,0], B supplies [0,10], C supplies [5,5]."
Calculate slowly: eighty percent of A is [8,0]. Ten percent of B is [0,1]. Ten percent of C is [0.5,0.5]. Sum them to get [8.5,1.5].
This output is gathered information for the querying position. It is not a generated token and not a replacement value vector.
V stays unchanged during the attention operation. The output goes through the rest of the block. The next layer receives updated representations and calculates its own Q, K and V.
If questions get deep, return to "mix information to update a representation."

=================================================================

SLIDE 7: Where do Q, K and V come from?
[3 minutes]

SAY: "In the first layer, token embeddings plus position information give us the representations. Learned transformations turn those representations into queries, keys, and values."
W_Q, W_K and W_V are learned matrices. Q, K and V are computed vectors. A key is not the same thing as the learned key matrix, and attention weights are yet another object: computed from Q and K.
The model learns its matrices during training: predict, measure error, update weights, repeat. During today's pretrained inference, those learned matrices are fixed, but Q/K/V depend on the input.
Later layers use the previous layer's contextual representations, not the original embedding again.
Do not teach the matrix multiplications now. Just point to the three learned transformations.

=================================================================

SLIDE 8: Self-attention: who is allowed to read whom?
[3 minutes]

SAY: "In self-attention, queries, keys and values come from the same sequence. Each row in these pictures is the position asking; columns are the positions available to read."
Point to the second row, love. An encoder can read I, love, learning and today. A causal decoder at love can only read I and love.
The diagonal is allowed because that position predicts the following token. At love, the target is learning, not love.
The model sets future attention scores to negative infinity before softmax, so their weights become zero. Gray squares mean not allowed, not merely small weights.
These are access diagrams, not measured attention heatmaps.
TRANSITION: "Now package this mechanism into a network."

SLIDE 9: A Transformer is more than attention
[3 minutes]

SAY: "Attention is the information-sharing operation. A Transformer is the larger architecture."
Walk through the cards. Position tells the model about order. Multi-head attention combines multiple learned views. The feed-forward network transforms features at each position. Residual connections add an existing representation to an update; normalization helps regulate feature scales.
Stacking blocks repeats these operations with different learned weights. The attention output is processed by the rest of its block before the next layer makes fresh Q/K/V.
No equations needed. Heads are not manually assigned grammar roles.
The cards describe roles, not an exact sequential ordering. Pre-norm and post-norm architectures arrange normalization differently.
TRANSITION: "How are the blocks arranged to read or generate text?"

=================================================================

SLIDE 10: Encoder reads. Decoder generates.
[2 minutes]

SAY: "The encoder builds representations of an input, such as a sentence to translate. The decoder generates output tokens."
Follow the left column down. Follow the right column down. The sideways arrow supplies encoder information to decoder cross-attention. The decoder's queries consult encoder keys and values.
Encoder-only: BERT-like representations. Decoder-only: GPT-like generation, without a separate encoder or encoder cross-attention. Encoder-decoder: translation and similar input-to-output tasks.
Explain roles only. This slide omits Add & Norm and repeated blocks intentionally. If asked, those are the components from the previous slide.
Do not imply all encoders use recurrent bottlenecks or that GPT has a separate encoder.

SLIDE 11: Generation: predict, append, repeat
[2 minutes]

SAY: "Attention itself does not output a word. After all the blocks, a vocabulary projection creates scores. Softmax turns those scores into probabilities. A decoding rule chooses a token."
All probabilities here are illustrative. They do not come from a real model run. Coffee has forty percent, tea twenty-five percent, the remaining vocabulary together thirty-five percent.
Choose coffee, append it to I like, then predict another token using I like coffee. It is a token, not necessarily a whole word.
Training can process many known positions in parallel. Generating new text still proceeds token by token.
Fluent text is not a guarantee of factual accuracy. Do not promise the capital-of-France example will answer Paris.

SLIDE 12: You only need three ideas
[3 minutes]

Spend the last three minutes on recap and questions.
SAY: "Attention matches queries with keys, then mixes values. Its output is a contextual vector, not a word. A Transformer organizes that operation into blocks that can support input representations or text generation."
Ask the room: "What is mixed, keys or values?" Values.
"Does the attention output directly become a generated word?" No.
"Why hide future tokens?" To prevent looking at the target while predicting it.
If someone asks how vectors get meaning, say training learns transformations useful for reducing prediction error; dimensions do not necessarily have human-readable labels.
If someone asks what the next layer receives, say updated token representations after the entire preceding block, not a raw new V.
Optional further reading after class: Attention Is All You Need, https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762 ; The Illustrated Transformer, https://jalammar.github.io/illustrated-transformer/ . All slide illustrations are original; numerical examples are illustrative.
